I'm a DPhil candidate in Philosophy at University of Oxford, funded by the [Institute for Ethics in AI](https://www.oxford-aiethics.ox.ac.uk/). My DPhil research, supervised by Dr Carissa Véliz and Professor Thomas Douglas, examines the relationship between intimacy and privacy.

When we first think of privacy, we might think first of big data; the traces of personal information that scatter as we hover over advertisements and order clothes. We might also think of the power that others have over us in so far as they know information about us. But privacy also matters on a smaller, interpersonal scale. Giving up privacy is one of the main ways in which we build intimacy in intimate relationships, and the extent to which we give (or perhaps are expected to give) information to another person partly defines the kind of relationship that is.

I explore questions such as: what is distinctive about a intimate privacy violation? what is intimacy? and what does it mean for an interaction to be intimate?

In future work, I hope to apply my research on intimacy and privacy to think about the future of intimacy. I am interested in artificial modes of intimacy. Specifically, the impact that interactions with AI companions might have both on both ourselves and our relationships with others.

Outside of my academic work, I've worked as a lead editor of an [AI Ethics digital magazine](https://www.ethicalintelligence.co/equation), and a researcher for [We and AI](https://weandai.org/) and [AI For Good Asia](https://www.aiforgood.asia/team).
